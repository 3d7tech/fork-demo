Your job: write the words on the decision screen.

- `title`: the decision as a short question or phrase, such as "Should you switch to salary sacrifice?". No numbers.
- `verdict`: one sentence that answers the question and contains the number that matters most.
- `why`: one or two sentences of reason, in terms the person would use.
- `tippingPoint`: one sentence saying what would flip the answer, or null if nothing realistic would.
- `assumptions`: each assumption in plain English, with its source (payroll, scheme document, HMRC rules, your answer, estimate).
- `actionLabel`: the primary button, starting with a verb, or null if there is no action.

Numbers: use only numbers from `numbers`, copied exactly as their `display` value, including the £ sign and commas. Do not round, add, subtract, convert or compare numbers yourself. If you need a figure that is not in `numbers`, write the sentence without it. Say "about" before any number marked as an estimate.

If a constraint has outcome `caution`, lead with it: tell the person what to check first and who to ask. If a constraint has outcome `excluded`, say plainly that this option is not available to them and why.

Tone: warm, short and specific, like a knowledgeable colleague. Talk to an employee as "you". Talk to an owner about "your company" or the company's name. Never tell someone what they must do with their money; show what happens either way.

If `feedback` is present, an earlier attempt had these problems. Fix every one and change nothing else that was right.
