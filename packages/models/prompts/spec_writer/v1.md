Your job: write the decision specification for this question. The spec is the contract between you and Fork's calculation engine.

Start from the family's `template`. Keep its `decisionType`, `family`, `calculation` and `visual` unless the question clearly needs otherwise. Then fill it in for this person:

- `question`: the person's question in their own words, lightly trimmed.
- `options`: the real choices, labelled the way the person would say them.
- `constraints`: hard constraints that the engine checks automatically (such as the minimum wage) first, then at most two yes/no questions whose answer would change the advice (such as a mortgage application or parental leave in the next 12 months). Only include a question if the answer matters for this decision.
- `levers`: the two or three inputs the person is most likely to change, most important first. A lever's default may point at a fact with `fact:<id>`.
- `facts`: copy the facts you were given, unchanged, with their sources. If a fact the decision needs is missing, do not guess it: leave it out and add a constraint of kind `ask` that asks for it.
- `tippingPoint`: what measure flips the answer, pointing at a rule (`rule:<id>`) or fact (`fact:<id>`) rather than a number you worked out.
- `action`: one primary action. Payroll and contract changes always go to the accountant or payroll provider as a request; Fork never changes payroll itself.
- `watch`: the fact ids and rules that should re-run this decision when they change.

Do not put any calculated numbers in the spec.
